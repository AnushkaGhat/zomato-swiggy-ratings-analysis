Bangalore · Zomato Ratings Investigation

What actually decides a restaurant's rating?

9,215 unique Bangalore restaurants, deduplicated from 41k raw listings. Structural factors — cost, cuisine, location — turned out to explain far less than what people actually wrote in their reviews.

9,215 restaurants analyzed 41,376 raw listings deduplicated 3 notebooks · EDA → NLP → Model
01Rating spread

Ratings barely use the scale.

The 1–5 scale exists, but almost nobody uses the ends of it. The overwhelming majority of restaurants sit between 3.0 and 4.5 — Zomato ratings function less like an honest quality measure and more like a narrow band of "good enough" consensus.

02Price vs. rating

Cost matters — moderately, not decisively.

Average rating does trend upward with price, but the relationship is moderate, not strong. Plenty of budget spots out-rate expensive ones.

03The "seriousness" signal

Table booking is a proxy, not a cause.

Restaurants that allow table booking rate noticeably higher on average — but this isn't booking itself doing the work. It's a marker for a cluster of traits (upscale, sit-down, consistent service) that travel together.

04Cuisine & geography

Niche cuisines and upscale corridors cluster together.

Mediterranean, European, and Asian cuisines top the cuisine leaderboard — not mainstream North/South Indian. The same story repeats geographically: Lavelle Road, Koramangala 5th Block, Church Street — Bangalore's premium dining corridors — top the location leaderboard.

05Sentiment vs. stars

The star rating hides real signal.

Review sentiment (scored with VADER) and star rating only loosely agree. At every star level — even 4.5 — some reviews read as genuinely negative. The number on the label isn't the whole story.

Words disproportionately common in negative reviews (vs. positive ones) point mostly to delivery and fulfillment failures — not kitchen quality.

06The model verdict

Review-derived signals beat every structural factor.

A tuned Gradient Boosting model, trained on deduplicated data with richer review-based features, confirms it numerically: signals pulled from reviews and dish tags — not cost, cuisine, or location — carry the most predictive weight.

model accuracy = on held-out, fully deduplicated data, tuned via grid search — up from 60.4% with an untuned model. An earlier un-deduplicated run showed 82.5%, later traced to train/test leakage from duplicate restaurant listings and corrected here.
